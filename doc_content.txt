SIH 2026 – Problem Statement 26019
National Digital Platform for Research and Policy Innovation in Land Governance
Comprehensive Problem Understanding, Literature Survey, Novelty, Data, Architecture, Cost, Time and User-Impact Document
1. Executive Summary
PS 26019 proposes a secure, scalable and AI-enabled National Research and Policy Innovation Platform for land governance. The key problem is not the absence of land data. India already has substantial land-record digitization, cadastral mapping, GIS, satellite, research and policy initiatives. The major gap is fragmentation: data, research, geospatial evidence and policy information are distributed across systems and are difficult to convert into reusable evidence for policy experimentation and decision-making.
The proposed platform acts as an intelligence and research layer above existing systems. It connects land and geospatial datasets, research publications, policy documents, socioeconomic information and analytical tools through AI, GIS, knowledge retrieval and scenario-based policy analysis.
2. Problem Statement in Simple Terms
Imagine a government wants to decide where to build a new industrial corridor. Three possible routes may exist. To choose responsibly, officials need land records, cadastral maps, satellite imagery, land-use information, population data, roads, climate/hazard information, research papers and policy documents. Today these resources can be scattered across departments, portals, research institutions and datasets.
The platform brings these resources into one research and policy intelligence environment. A policymaker can ask a natural-language question, inspect the supporting evidence on a map, compare historical trends, and evaluate alternative scenarios before implementation.

6. Novelty
The novelty should NOT be presented as simply 'AI + GIS + land records'. The stronger novelty is integration into a research-to-policy loop.
Research-to-policy intelligence: connects academic evidence directly with land, geospatial and socioeconomic evidence.
AI Land Governance Research Copilot: retrieves and synthesizes source documents rather than providing unsupported generic answers.
Research-to-GIS connection: links a research finding to its geographic context, land-use layer and supporting spatial evidence.
Policy experimentation: enables scenario comparison before implementation rather than only reporting historical conditions.
Land Governance Knowledge Graph: connects policies, research, datasets, locations, indicators and outcomes.
Evidence-to-action pipeline: transforms fragmented information into traceable, evidence-backed decision support.
Ask-the-map experience: users can express spatial questions in natural language and receive corresponding map/analytics views.
7. Novelty Statement for SIH
Unlike existing land-information and GIS platforms that primarily store, visualize or administer land data, the proposed platform creates an AI-powered research-to-policy ecosystem that connects land data, geospatial evidence, academic knowledge and policy scenarios to support evidence-based land governance.
Short version: Existing systems tell us what is happening to land; the proposed platform helps users understand the evidence, explore why it is happening, compare what could happen next, and evaluate policy options before implementation.

14. Data Strategy
Open data first: prioritize public government, satellite, GIS and research datasets.
Synthetic data for sensitive fields: use synthetic cadastral/ownership-like structures for the prototype instead of personal data.
API-ready architecture: design connectors so authorized government datasets can be integrated later.
Metadata-first design: store source, date, geographic coverage, license/permission and data quality metadata.
Versioning: preserve dataset versions so analytical results remain reproducible.

24. Final Value Proposition
The proposed platform does not attempt to replace India's existing land-information infrastructure. It connects that infrastructure with research, geospatial evidence, socioeconomic context, AI-assisted knowledge discovery and policy experimentation. Its central value is converting fragmented land information into traceable evidence and scenario-based decision support.
25. One-Line Pitch
“From Land Data to Policy Evidence.” The platform connects land data, research, GIS and AI to help researchers and policymakers understand land-change patterns, evaluate evidence and compare policy scenarios before implementation.

27. Final Strategic Recommendation
For SIH, build a narrow but deep prototype: one region, one major land-governance use case, a curated research/policy corpus, one geospatial analysis pipeline and one policy simulation. Make every AI output traceable to evidence. Demonstrate measurable reduction in information-discovery and repetitive analysis effort. Present the production architecture as scalable and API-ready rather than pretending the hackathon prototype already contains all national land data.

31. Data Preprocessing Pipeline
Use a reproducible pipeline rather than manually editing files:
Raw Source → Download/Connector → Schema Validation → CRS Standardization → Missing-Value Handling → Spatial Join → Feature Engineering → Quality Checks → Versioned Storage → Model/Analytics Layer
Standardize all spatial data to a documented CRS; avoid mixing coordinate systems silently.
Keep original raw files untouched and create processed versions separately.
Record source, date accessed, spatial resolution, temporal coverage and license/permission.
For raster data, clip to the pilot area and use cloud masking for optical imagery.
Use consistent class labels for LULC across years before calculating change.
Do not combine datasets at incompatible spatial scales without documenting aggregation or interpolation.
Keep a data-quality score for every major dataset.

34. Model Training Prompt / Specification
Use the following as the master instruction for the AI/data-science team when implementing the predictive model:
MASTER MODEL DEVELOPMENT PROMPTYou are the lead geospatial machine-learning engineer for a national land-governance decision-support platform. Build a production-quality, reproducible and explainable land-use change / urban expansion prediction pipeline for a clearly defined pilot region.OBJECTIVEPredict the probability that each spatial unit will undergo a specified land-use transition (for example, agricultural → built-up) during the next analysis period, and produce a calibrated risk/probability map that can be used as one input to policy scenario analysis.DATAUse only documented datasets with provenance. Combine historical LULC labels, satellite-derived spectral indices, infrastructure proximity, population/socioeconomic indicators, climate/hazard indicators and terrain/context variables where available.REQUIREMENTS1. Perform rigorous spatial and temporal data validation.2. Harmonize CRS, resolution, spatial extent and temporal coverage.3. Prevent data leakage: future-year variables must never enter training features for a past prediction.4. Split train/validation/test data spatially and/or temporally so that nearby pixels or polygons do not create unrealistic leakage.5. Establish a simple baseline before advanced models.6. Train at least two candidate models and compare them using appropriate metrics.7. Report precision, recall, F1, ROC-AUC/PR-AUC where appropriate, calibration error and confusion matrix for classification.8. For spatial predictions, report spatially meaningful validation results and visually inspect error hotspots.9. Calibrate probabilities when necessary.10. Use SHAP or another suitable explainability method to identify major drivers.11. Provide uncertainty/confidence with every prediction; do not output unsupported certainty.12. Save preprocessing, feature definitions, model version, training period, dataset versions and evaluation results.13. Create reproducible scripts/configuration for training and inference.14. Export prediction layers in a GIS-compatible format.15. Build an inference API that accepts a region/time/scenario and returns predictions plus explanations.16. Include data-quality checks and fail safely when required features are missing.17. Document limitations and assumptions.18. Never present model output as a guaranteed future outcome. It is decision-support evidence.MODEL SELECTIONStart with Random Forest as a robust baseline. Compare with XGBoost/LightGBM if suitable. Use a deep-learning remote-sensing model only when labelled imagery and sufficient training data justify it.DELIVERABLES- cleaned feature dataset- preprocessing pipeline- trained baseline model- trained candidate model- validation report- calibration report- explainability report- prediction GeoTIFF/GeoPackage/GeoJSON as appropriate- model metadata- inference API- reproducible training script- model card describing intended use, limitations, data sources and known risksQUALITY BARThe model must be reproducible, spatially validated, explainable, calibrated where possible, versioned and conservative in its claims. Optimize for trustworthy decision support, not only the highest single accuracy number.
35. RAG / Research Copilot Master Prompt
Use this prompt for the land-governance research assistant:
MASTER RAG PROMPTYou are an evidence-grounded Land Governance Research Copilot. Answer only from retrieved and authorized documents/data. Never invent laws, statistics, research findings, policy provisions, dataset values or citations.For every answer:1. Identify the user's research question and geographic scope.2. Retrieve relevant research papers, government documents, policy documents and dataset metadata.3. Prefer authoritative government and primary research sources.4. Rank sources by relevance, authority, date and geographic applicability.5. Clearly distinguish observed facts, research findings, model estimates and assumptions.6. Cite each material claim to its source document or dataset.7. If evidence conflicts, explicitly show the conflict and explain possible reasons.8. If evidence is insufficient, say so.9. For GIS questions, return the relevant spatial layer/entity and the evidence supporting it.10. For policy questions, summarize policy objective, jurisdiction, target population/area, evidence of outcomes and limitations.11. Never provide a definitive policy decision. Provide evidence and scenario options for human review.12. Return a concise answer first, followed by evidence, sources, assumptions and limitations.OUTPUT FORMAT- Answer- Key Evidence- Relevant Locations / GIS Layers- Relevant Policies- Relevant Research- Confidence / Evidence Quality- Assumptions- Limitations- Sources
36. Policy Simulation Prompt / Specification
POLICY SCENARIO ENGINE PROMPTYou are a policy-analysis engine for land governance. Compare alternative scenarios using only validated baseline data and explicitly supplied assumptions.For each scenario:1. Define the intervention and geographic scope.2. State all assumptions.3. Identify affected land-use classes and populations.4. Estimate changes in selected indicators.5. Provide uncertainty ranges where the model supports them.6. Compare against a baseline/no-policy scenario.7. Show positive and negative impacts.8. Identify potentially affected groups/areas.9. Link each major assumption to its source or evidence.10. Never claim causal certainty when the model only provides association or scenario projection.11. Return a transparent comparison table and GIS-ready output.12. Mark the result as decision support requiring expert/government validation.

41. Important Data-Access Notes
Bhuvan exposes multiple thematic layers and services, including LULC at different scales and periods; some data products have specific download/request procedures. Check the dataset's stated access conditions before redistribution. 
Bhuvan notes that georeferenced cadastral data in its portal is for planning/development purposes and is not valid for legal measurements or regulatory applications. Do not present it as authoritative legal ownership data.
Copernicus states that Sentinel data are available on a free, full and open basis subject to its terms; use the official Data Space for current access.
For government land records, use only authorized/public information. Do not scrape or expose personally identifiable ownership information.
For research papers, respect copyright and repository/licensing conditions. Store metadata and permitted text; do not assume every PDF can be redistributed.
For prototype satellite processing, downloading only the pilot region and selected time periods is much more practical than creating a national archive.

43. Final End-to-End Model Architecture
Data Sources → Data Lake/Object Storage → ETL/Validation → PostgreSQL + PostGIS → Feature Store/Analytics → ML Models + RAG + Knowledge Graph → Policy Scenario Engine → GIS/Decision Dashboard → Human Review
The AI layer should therefore contain three cooperating intelligence systems: (1) predictive geospatial ML, (2) evidence-grounded research/policy RAG, and (3) transparent scenario simulation. This is stronger than using a generic LLM as the entire system.

45. Final Positioning
The project should be presented as a national-scale architecture demonstrated through a focused pilot. The prototype does not need every land dataset in India. Its strength should be the ability to combine heterogeneous evidence, analyze it spatially, retrieve research with citations, model a defined land-use transition and compare policy scenarios transparently.
